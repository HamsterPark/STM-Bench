# Fidelity card

What the simulated instrument models, where each part comes from, how it is checked, and where
it is known to differ from a real microscope. A result on STM-Bench is a result on this
instrument; this card is what lets a reader judge how far it carries.

Source labels: **fit** — fitted to the maintainer's lab data (the corpus is private; counts and
dates are given); **lit** — a published relation or value; **model** — a modelling assumption
chosen for plausibility, not measured. Checks name the tests in `tests/` unless stated.
Design notes in Chinese: [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) §5 and §9.

| The trace of a move (`move_tip`, every move, whatever the tip's state): the Z controller's output and the measured current at every 0.05 nm of the path (coarser beyond 50 nm: at most 1000 samples, each the mean over its stretch). The height under the tip is the tip as it is, every apex (a double tip shows two signatures), over the surface with every atom *where it was at that sample* (the drag records the path index of each hop and pick-up). Atoms are drawn as a frame draws them: the Gaussian bump blurred by the tip's size (height × σ²/(σ²+s²), width √(σ²+s²), s = √(R/2κ)), so an atom has the same apparent height in a trace and in a frame (with `tip_anisotropy` the blur is the renderer's ellipse and the same holds); the substrate is blurred along the path by the width the blur has in the path's direction, the atomic lattice is not. Feedback on: the Z loop follows the height to the gap `equilibrium_gap` gives (the loop discretised exactly per sample, as a 0.2 s sample is thousands of its time constants) and the current is what the junction carries across the gap that results, through the preamplifier clamp (a manipulation current above the selected range reads its limit all along); pressed into the sample (`crash_over_range`, the row above): the target is contact and the current is the clamp, as the status reading has it; off: Z held, the current follows the height; withdrawn: the retract limit and no current. **Noise: a sample is the mean over its dwell** (0.2 s at 0.25 nm/s, 12.5 ms at 4 nm/s), as the controller records it while the tip crosses its step, not one reading. The white parts (Z's 2 pm, the preamplifier's floor and the shot noise) fall as 1/√(dwell / 2.29 ms), 2.29 ms being the pixel dwell of the default scan (0.586 s per line of 256 pixels): a scan keeps its figures whatever its pixel dwell, so that is the one dwell they are tied to, and a sample shorter than that keeps the scan's figures; a mains or pump line averages as a sinusoid (× sinc(f·dwell)). What a mean does not remove stays: the 1/f current noise, a slow wander of Z (0.3 pm rms over the record, 1/f), what a tip that keeps changing adds to Z in a scan (30 λ times the floor, kept at its size and drawn on its own) and a crashed tip's flicker. A bare trace at 0.25 nm/s carries 0.37 pm of Z noise (0.21 white, 0.3 slow) and 0.034 pA on the current at a 200 pA setpoint, at 100 nm/s the scan's 2 pm and 0.26 pA. All of it comes from the trace's own stream (`[seed, 0x7ACE]`): no other stream gains a draw. A pick-up takes the atom's bump off the surface at its sample (a step down; the tip is not lengthened by it, as `Tip.pick_up` does not in a frame); a change of the tip during a move is a step in the trace, with the tip as that event left it: at the time the tip's log gives it where the move tunnelled through an interval (the hazards and recoveries outside scans), at the start for the plough of a pressed tip, and at a time drawn from that stream for the change a drag stamps at its start (the drag itself is unchanged by it) | model (Gaussian bump and loop: the renderer's; the mean over a dwell: white noise) + **assumption** (the slow Z wander of 0.3 pm: no measurement behind it; the reading of hops is the same for it from 0 to 1 pm) + lit (an atom pulled by the tip gives a sawtooth in Z with the lattice period: Bartels, Meyer & Rieder, PRL 79, 697 (1997)) | `test_move_trace.py` (steps = hops with the noise off, an atom's profile in a trace against the same line of a frame to 1 pm for a round tip and three elliptical ones, contact and the clamp, tip changes at the times of the tip's log, streams unchanged against the same moves without a trace; the noise: the 1/√dwell law on 40 000 draws, what stays, composition, and the noise of a real trace at 0.25 and 100 nm/s as the difference between two worlds of one seed), `test_instrument.py` (replay), `test_export.py`, `test_grade.py`; read back by `stmbench.analysis.trace` (`test_analysis_trace.py`) | **Tooth height is not calibrated.** A tooth, 3–5 pm for a good tip (the bump falls by a tenth between hops; 1–2 pm is the median jump at a hop in a sample, 4–13 pm the 90th percentile, and 10–25 pm for a hop after a long lag), follows from the drag model's hop criterion (`AdatomRegistry.drag`: an attempt when the tip lags the atom by 0.6 nearest-neighbour distances, 0.15 nm on Cu(111)) and the imaging bump; it was not compared with measured pulling-mode tip-height curves (the sawtooth of Bartels et al. (1997) may have larger jumps; no figure of theirs was checked here), so real teeth may be larger. With the noise of a mean over the dwell (0.4–0.9 pm per sample at 0.25–4 nm/s) a single tooth is a 2–4 noise step and a train a faint ripple; the clearest signatures are the fall of the bump when the atom is left behind (from the start with no hop, about n lattice spacings later after n hops) and the large jump of a late hop (an atom dropped at its first attempt and caught again at the edge of the tip's reach hops from a long lag). The 2.29 ms reference dwell and the 0.3 pm slow wander are the model's choices, not measurements; a scan pixel keeps its noise as it was. The path is the drag's, in sample coordinates fixed by the drift at the move's start (during a move drift acts along Z only); the substrate is blurred along the path rather than over the plane; a hop's current spike (milliseconds) is not resolved at 0.2 s per sample; an open-loop move cannot crash into a step; the junction (barrier, gap) is the one the move began with |

## What this means for scores

- Claims that turn on a modelled quantity with a literature basis (periods, band parameters,
  force-law parameters) are as credible as that relation; claims that turn on modelled
  behaviour (manipulation thresholds, tip-change rates) test operation against this model.
- Tip-diagnosis statements can be scored only since the 2026-10-13 blur fix.
- The simulator's images are cleaner than real ones in places (no thermal noise beyond the Z
  floor, no tip-shape convolution beyond the Gaussian): perception results on it are an upper
  bound for the same perception on real data.
